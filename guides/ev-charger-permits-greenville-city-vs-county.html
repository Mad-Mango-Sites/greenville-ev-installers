EV Charger Permits: City of Greenville vs Greenville County

Every Level 2 charger install needs an electrical permit and an inspection. The tricky part in Greenville is knowing which office issues it, because a mailing address doesn't show if a home is inside city limits.

Why the permit matters

  • It's required for new 240-volt circuits.
  • An inspector checks the work, which protects you and your insurance.
  • Duke's Charger Prep Credit asks for a copy of the approved permit.

How to tell which office you need

  1. Look up your parcel on Greenville County's property records, or check your property tax bill. If it lists a city, you're inside those limits.
  2. Ask your installer. They file permits all over the county and can usually tell from the address.
  3. If you're in an unincorporated community such as Taylors or Five Forks, it's Greenville County.

Who pulls the permit?

Your licensed installer, in almost every case. If an installer suggests skipping the permit, choose someone else.

How long it takes

It varies by office and season. A simple residential electrical permit is often quick. Panel upgrades also need the utility to disconnect and reconnect power, which adds time. Ask your installer for a realistic timeline.
