Mauldin EV Charger Permit Guide

Last Updated On: November 19, 2026

Installing a Level 2 charger at a Mauldin home means adding electrical wiring, and that needs a permit and an inspection. First you need to know which office issues it, since a Mauldin mailing address doesn't always mean you're inside city limits. This guide covers who issues the permit, how to apply, what it costs, how inspections work, and how the permit ties into Duke Energy's Charger Prep Credit.

For the other towns in the county, see our permits overview: city vs county. The Greenville EV charger permit guide covers the county process in the same format.

Do you need a permit for a home EV charger in Mauldin?

Yes. Mauldin says a permit is required "before adding, altering, or replacing any electrical" system, and that contractors must have the permit in hand before work starts (City of Mauldin: Permit Applications & Fee Schedules). Greenville County lists "all electrical" work as needing a permit (Greenville County Building Safety: Permits).

City of Mauldin or Greenville County: who issues your permit?

It depends on whether the property is inside city limits.

  • Inside Mauldin city limits: the City of Mauldin Business & Development Services department issues the permit.
  • Outside city limits, in unincorporated Greenville County: Greenville County Building Safety issues it.

How to check if you're inside city limits

  • Call Business & Development Services at (864) 234-3475 with your address.
  • Look up your parcel and tax map number on Greenville County Real Property Search. Mauldin's trade permit application asks for the parcel ID, and the county permit needs the tax map number too.
  • Your installer can usually tell you, since they pull permits across the county.

How to apply for an EV charger permit in the City of Mauldin

  1. Confirm you're in city limits with the city or your installer.
  2. Apply online through Citizenserve. Mauldin takes applications and payments through its Citizenserve portal. An electrical charger circuit on an existing home is a stand-alone trade permit.
  3. List the job cost. Mauldin prices trade permits on the contract cost (see fees below). The application also asks which utility serves the address.
  4. Pay the fee online, or by cash, check or credit card at the office.
  5. Schedule and pass inspection. Call the city's inspection request line at (864) 289-8976 (City of Mauldin Directory).

Who files it? Usually your electrician. Mauldin says contractors must be licensed, bonded and insured. The city also posts a Home Owner Builder Disclosure form for homeowners doing their own work, on the same permits page.

Contact: Business & Development Services, 5 E. Butler Rd., Mauldin, SC 29662. Phone (864) 234-3475 (City of Mauldin: Permit Applications & Fee Schedules).

How to apply in unincorporated Greenville County

  1. Find your tax map number. The county says every permit application needs either a tax map number or an active permit number for the site (Greenville County Permits). Look it up on Greenville County Real Property Search.
  2. Apply through eTRAKiT. The county says all permits must be submitted through eTRAKiT. Setup steps are in the county's eTRAKiT user manual.
  3. Wait for review. A permit technician checks the application for floodplain, road frontage, setbacks and completeness.
  4. Get the permit issued. The county currently lists a five-day processing period.
  5. Schedule and pass inspection. County inspectors take calls daily from 8:00 to 8:30 a.m. and inspect from 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. (Greenville County Inspections).

Who files it? If a contractor does the work, the contractor (an SC license holder) must submit and sign the application. If you do the work yourself as the owner, you submit it, and owner-builder permits come with conditions listed on the county's page.

Which power company serves you, and does Duke's Charger Prep Credit apply?

Mauldin's own trade permit application lists two power companies for city addresses: Duke Energy and Laurens Electric (City of Mauldin: Permit Applications). Duke Energy Carolinas serves the Upstate, including Greenville County (Duke Energy news release), and Laurens Electric Cooperative serves parts of Greenville County, with a drive-through office on East Butler Road in Mauldin. The name on your power bill tells you which one you have. See which utility serves you.

Duke Energy's Charger Prep Credit

If Duke is your utility, its Charger Prep Credit pays back part of the cost of getting your home ready for a Level 2 charger. It's a Duke program, so Laurens Electric members don't qualify. Per Duke's page:

CostCounts toward the credit?
Conduit, wiring and outlets supplying the chargerYes
Panel upgrades and breaker installationYes
EV charger hardwareNo
Hardware mounting and commissioningNo
Pulling permits and feesNo

There are two ways to get it:

  • Customer Credit Option: you pick your electrician, then submit the paid invoice to Duke and get a check. Duke says this covers prep work done in the last four months (Customer Credit Option).
  • Contractor Credit Option: Duke helps you choose an electrical contractor, and the credit shows up as a discount on the contractor's final bill (Contractor Credit Option).

Your permit matters here. Duke's latest program report to the SC Public Service Commission says applications are marked invalid without a detailed invoice with separate labor and materials lines, a copy of the approved permit from the local permitting authority, a summary of any grant funding, and proof of EV registration. It also says the service address has to match the address on the EV registration (Duke semi-annual update to the SC PSC, Sept. 2026). Keep a copy of your approved permit.

Duke's page doesn't publish the dollar amount. Ask Duke or your installer for the current credit before you count on it. Read more in our Duke Charger Prep Credit guide.

FAQ

Do I pull the permit or does my electrician?

Usually your electrician, who must be licensed, bonded and insured and have the permit in hand before starting. Homeowners doing their own work use the city's Home Owner Builder Disclosure form.

How do I know if my Mauldin address is in the city?

Call Business & Development Services at (864) 234-3475, or ask your installer.

How much is an EV charger permit in Mauldin?

Mauldin's posted schedule sets a $30.00 minimum for a stand-alone residential trade permit. Fees are doubled if work starts before the permit is issued.

How much is it in unincorporated Greenville County?

The county's minimum electrical permit fee is $72.00, with $3.60 per circuit and $0.36 per amp of service on new or service-change permits.

How long does a permit take?

Greenville County currently lists a five-day processing period. Mauldin doesn't publish one, so ask your installer.

I'm a Laurens Electric member. Can I get Duke's credit?

No. The Charger Prep Credit is a Duke Energy program. Call Laurens Electric and ask about its EV programs.

Does Duke's credit pay for the permit?

No. Duke's page says pulling permits and permit fees don't count toward the Charger Prep Credit. You still need the approved permit to apply.
