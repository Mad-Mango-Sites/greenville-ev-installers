Duke Energy's Charger Prep Credit in South Carolina

Duke Energy will pay part of the cost of preparing your home for a Level 2 charger. You may see a higher cap quoted online. That's the cap for Duke Energy Progress customers. Greenville and the rest of the Upstate are served by Duke Energy Carolinas, where the home cap is $1,236. Here's how it works and how to claim it.

Who qualifies

  • You're a Duke Energy customer in South Carolina. Customers of Greer CPW, Easley Combined Utilities, Blue Ridge Electric or Laurens Electric aren't eligible.
  • You're installing a Level 2 (or faster) charger at your home or business.
  • For homes: you have an EV registered at the same address as your Duke account.
  • The work is done by a licensed electrician, with a permit.

How much you can get

The credit is the lower of your actual prep costs or Duke's estimate of the extra electricity revenue from your charger over five years. Duke's South Carolina tariffs set these caps (based on rates effective April 1, 2023):

Customer typeDuke Energy Carolinas (Upstate, including Greenville)Duke Energy Progress
Home, Level 2$1,236See Duke's Leaf No. 741
Multifamily, per Level 2 charger$1,757See Duke's Leaf No. 741

What it covers

Per Duke's Charger Prep Credit page:

CostCounts toward the credit?
Conduit, wiring and the outlet that feeds the chargerYes
Panel upgrades and breaker installationYes
The charger itselfNo
Mounting and commissioning the chargerNo
Permit feesNo

Two ways to claim it

Customer Credit Option (you apply)

Hire any licensed electrician. After the work is done, apply to Duke with:

  • a detailed invoice with labor and materials on separate lines;
  • a copy of the approved permit;
  • proof of EV registration;
  • a summary of any other grant money you received.

Apply within 120 days of the later of the invoice date or your EV registration date. Duke sends a check.

Contractor Credit Option (the installer applies)

Use a Duke-approved contractor and the credit comes off your bill from the contractor. Duke says it will help you choose an electrical contractor. Duke doesn't publish the list of approved contractors that we could find, so we can't tell you which installers on this site are approved. Ask each one.

Tips to avoid a rejected application

  • Ask for an itemized invoice up front. Without separate labor and materials lines, Duke treats the application as incomplete.
  • Make sure the address on your EV registration matches your Duke account.
  • Keep the permit. Duke asks for a copy.
  • Don't wait. The tariff sets a 120-day deadline.

For businesses and property owners

Business customers apply directly. There's no contractor discount option, and you'll fill out a Customer Usage Profile describing how the chargers will be used. Multi-charger and DC fast projects also need a schematic.
