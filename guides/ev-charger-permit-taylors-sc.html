Taylors EV Charger Permit Guide

Last Updated On: November 19, 2026

Installing a Level 2 charger at a Taylors home means adding electrical wiring, and that needs a permit and an inspection. Taylors is simpler than most towns here: it isn't an incorporated city, so there's no town permit office. Greenville County issues the permit. This guide covers how to apply, what it costs, how inspections work, and how the permit ties into Duke Energy's Charger Prep Credit.

For the other towns in the county, see our permits overview: city vs county. The Greenville EV charger permit guide covers the same county process alongside the City of Greenville.

Do you need a permit for a home EV charger in Taylors?

Yes. Greenville County lists "all electrical" work as needing a permit (Greenville County Building Safety: Permits). A new 240-volt circuit for a charger is electrical work, so it needs a permit and an inspection.

Who issues your permit in Taylors?

Taylors is an unincorporated community, so homes there are in unincorporated Greenville County.

  • Taylors addresses in unincorporated Greenville County: Greenville County Building Safety issues the permit.
  • If your property turns out to be inside a city's limits: that city issues it instead.

How to check your address before you apply

  • Look up your parcel and tax map number on Greenville County Real Property Search. You'll need the tax map number for the county permit anyway.
  • Your installer can usually tell you too, since they pull permits across the county.

How to apply for an EV charger permit in Taylors (Greenville County)

  1. Find your tax map number. The county says every permit application needs either a tax map number or an active permit number for the site (Greenville County Permits). Look it up on Greenville County Real Property Search.
  2. Apply through eTRAKiT. The county says all permits must be submitted through eTRAKiT. Setup steps are in the county's eTRAKiT user manual.
  3. Wait for review. A permit technician checks the application for floodplain, road frontage, setbacks and completeness.
  4. Get the permit issued. The county currently lists a five-day processing period.
  5. Schedule and pass inspection. County inspectors take calls daily from 8:00 to 8:30 a.m. and inspect from 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. (Greenville County Inspections).

Who files it? If a contractor does the work, the contractor (an SC license holder) must submit and sign the application. If you do the work yourself as the owner, you submit it, and owner-builder permits come with conditions listed on the county's page.

Contact: Greenville County Building Safety. See the Permits and Inspections pages for current contacts.

Which power company serves you, and does Duke's Charger Prep Credit apply?

Duke Energy Carolinas serves the Upstate, including Greenville County (Duke Energy news release). Blue Ridge Electric Cooperative and Laurens Electric Cooperative also serve parts of Greenville County. The name on your power bill tells you which one you have. See which utility serves you.

Duke Energy's Charger Prep Credit

If Duke is your utility, its Charger Prep Credit pays back part of the cost of getting your home ready for a Level 2 charger. It's a Duke program, so co-op members don't qualify. Per Duke's page:

CostCounts toward the credit?
Conduit, wiring and outlets supplying the chargerYes
Panel upgrades and breaker installationYes
EV charger hardwareNo
Hardware mounting and commissioningNo
Pulling permits and feesNo

There are two ways to get it:

  • Customer Credit Option: you pick your electrician, then submit the paid invoice to Duke and get a check. Duke says this covers prep work done in the last four months (Customer Credit Option).
  • Contractor Credit Option: Duke helps you choose an electrical contractor, and the credit shows up as a discount on the contractor's final bill (Contractor Credit Option).

Your permit matters here. Duke's latest program report to the SC Public Service Commission says applications are marked invalid without a detailed invoice with separate labor and materials lines, a copy of the approved permit from the local permitting authority, a summary of any grant funding, and proof of EV registration. It also says the service address has to match the address on the EV registration (Duke semi-annual update to the SC PSC, Sept. 2026). Keep a copy of your approved permit.

Duke's page doesn't publish the dollar amount. Ask Duke or your installer for the current credit before you count on it. Read more in our Duke Charger Prep Credit guide.

FAQ

Do I pull the permit or does my electrician?

Usually your electrician. In Greenville County, a contractor doing the work must submit and sign the application. Homeowners can pull their own permit under the county's owner-builder rules.

Does Taylors have its own permit office?

No. Taylors isn't an incorporated city, so Greenville County Building Safety handles the permit.

How much is an EV charger permit in Taylors?

The county's minimum electrical permit fee is $72.00 per permit, with $3.60 per circuit and $0.36 per amp of service on new or service-change permits.

How long does a permit take?

Greenville County currently lists a five-day processing period.

How do inspections work?

County inspectors take calls daily from 8:00 to 8:30 a.m. and inspect from 8:30 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. From Nov 1, 2026, residential alteration permits have one rough-in and one final inspection.

I'm a Blue Ridge or Laurens Electric member. Can I get Duke's credit?

No. The Charger Prep Credit is a Duke Energy program. If your bill comes from a co-op, call it and ask about EV programs.

Does Duke's credit pay for the permit?

No. Duke's page says pulling permits and permit fees don't count toward the Charger Prep Credit. You still need the approved permit to apply.
