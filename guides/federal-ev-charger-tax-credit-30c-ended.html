The Federal EV Charger Tax Credit (30C) Has Ended

If you're reading older articles about a 30% federal tax credit for home chargers, that credit is gone for new installs. Here's what changed and what's still available locally.

What happened

The Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Property Credit, known as 30C, used to cover 30% of the cost of a home charger and its installation, up to $1,000, in eligible census tracts. The law passed in July 2025 (Public Law 119-21) moved its end date from 2032 to June 30, 2026. The IRS says the credit isn't allowed for property placed in service after June 30, 2026.

If your charger was installed before July 1, 2026

You may still be able to claim it on that year's return with IRS Form 8911, if your home was in an eligible census tract and you meet the other rules. Talk to a tax professional. We can't give tax advice.

What's still available

  • Duke Energy's Charger Prep Credit for Duke customers: up to $1,236 for most Upstate homes.
  • Your utility's own programs, if it's not Duke.

Watch out for outdated quotes

If an installer's quote or website still mentions a federal tax credit for a new install, ask them about it. It may be old copy.
