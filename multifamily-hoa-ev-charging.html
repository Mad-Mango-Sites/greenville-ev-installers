EV Charging for Apartments, Condos and HOAs in Greenville

Shared parking is the hardest place to add charging. Somebody has to own the equipment, decide who pays for the power, and make sure the building's electrical service can handle it. This page is for property managers, HOA boards and residents who want to start that conversation.

How these projects usually go

  1. Survey interest. Find out how many residents have or plan to buy an EV.
  2. Check the electrical capacity. A contractor looks at the building's service and where chargers could go.
  3. Start small, with room to grow. Run conduit for more spaces than you need now, and install a few chargers first.
  4. Decide how billing works. Networked chargers can bill each driver. Simpler setups put chargers on a resident's own meter.

Duke's multifamily credit

For Duke Energy Carolinas customers in South Carolina, the Charger Prep Credit has a multifamily category, capped at $1,757 per Level 2 charger (based on rates effective April 1, 2023, per Duke's SC tariff). The property owner applies directly. See the Duke credit guide.
