What to know about charging in Greenville

City limits are smaller than the mailing area. Plenty of homes with a Greenville address sit in unincorporated Greenville County. That changes who issues your permit, not who can do the work. Your installer can tell you which applies, or you can look up your parcel on the county's site.

Older neighborhoods, older panels. Homes in North Main, Augusta Road, Alta Vista near Cleveland Park, and the city's historic districts such as Overbrook, Hampton-Pinckney and West End were built long before anyone planned for a car charger. Some still run on smaller electrical service or a panel with no open spaces. Ask for a load calculation before you buy a charger. A smart charger set to a lower amperage, or a load-management device, can sometimes avoid a full upgrade.

Historic districts. If you live in one of the city's preservation districts and want the charger outside where it's visible from the street, ask the city whether design review applies before you mount anything.

Detached garages and carports. Many older lots have a garage at the back of the property. A long trench or wire run is often the biggest cost on these jobs, so get it priced separately.

Greenville FAQ

Do I pull the permit or does the installer?

The installer normally does, since the permit is tied to the licensed contractor doing the work. Inside city limits it goes through the City of Greenville; outside, through Greenville County.

I live downtown in a condo. Can I get a charger?

Usually, but it depends on your building's parking and electrical room, and the HOA or property manager has to approve it. See our page on apartments, condos and HOAs.
