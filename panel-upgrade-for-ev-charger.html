Panel Upgrades for EV Chargers in Greenville, SC

A Level 2 charger is one of the biggest loads in a house. If your panel is small, full or old, it may need work first. That doesn't always mean a new 200-amp service. Sometimes a smaller charger setting, a sub-panel or a load-management device does the job for less. These installers list panel work as part of their EV services.

Signs you may need panel work

  • Your main breaker says 100 amps or less.
  • There are no open spaces in the panel for a 2-pole breaker.
  • Breakers already trip when the dryer, oven and HVAC run together.
  • The panel is a brand an electrician tells you to replace.

Ask for a load calculation first

A load calculation looks at what's already on your panel and how much is left for a charger. Ask every installer you talk to for one. If two quotes disagree on whether you need an upgrade, the load calculation shows you why.

The Duke credit can help here

If you're a Duke Energy customer, panel upgrades and breaker work count toward the Charger Prep Credit, along with wiring, conduit and the outlet. For most Upstate homes the cap is $1,236. The application needs a detailed invoice with labor and materials listed separately, so ask your installer for that format. See the Duke credit guide.
