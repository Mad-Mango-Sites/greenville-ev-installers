A month is 30.4 days (365 ÷ 12).

How the estimate works

  • Miles a month = miles per day × 30.4 days.
  • Electricity = miles a month ÷ miles per kWh. If you enter kWh/100 mi, it's miles a month × kWh/100 mi ÷ 100.
  • Cost to charge at home = electricity (kWh) × Duke Energy Carolinas' Schedule RS energy charge for the block you picked.
  • Gas for the same miles = miles a month ÷ MPG × gas price.
  • Difference = gas cost − home charging cost.

Where the numbers come from

Electricity rate13.8125¢/kWh for the first 1,000 kWh used per month and 14.4661¢/kWh for all over 1,000 kWh. Duke Energy Carolinas, LLC, Schedule RS, Residential Service (South Carolina), SC Fifty-Ninth Revised Leaf No. 11, effective for service rendered on and after March 1, 2026 (PSCSC Docket No. 2025-172-E, Order No. 2025-769). Checked Oct 21, 2026.
Rate blocksSchedule RS has two energy-charge blocks and no seasonal or time-of-day prices. Charging adds kWh on top of what your home already uses, so the calculator uses the first-block rate unless you say your home already uses more than 1,000 kWh a month. If your charging pushes your home over 1,000 kWh, part of it is billed at the higher rate, so the real cost falls between the two.
Gas price (default)$3.833/gal, AAA's current South Carolina average for regular, as of Oct 21, 2026 (AAA gas prices, South Carolina). Change it to what you pay.
MPG (default)25.6 mpg, the average estimated real-world fuel economy of model year 2024 new vehicles without BEVs and PHEVs, from the 2025 EPA Automotive Trends Report (EPA-420-R-26-001, February 2026). Change it to your own car's MPG.
EV efficiencyNo default. Efficiency varies a lot by model, so enter your own car's rating.

What this estimate leaves out: Duke's $11.96 monthly basic customer charge (you pay it with or without an EV); the riders the tariff adds per kWh (Energy Efficiency, EDIT-1, PC, PTC, CARE and Storm Securitization); the fuel, variable environmental, avoided capacity and DERP adjustments and the DERP fixed monthly charge; sales tax and municipal or other government fees; time-of-use and other Duke rate schedules; energy lost while charging; public charging; and the cost of the charger and its installation. Utilities other than Duke Energy Carolinas (Greer CPW, Easley Combined Utilities, Blue Ridge Electric, Laurens Electric) have their own rates. Which utility serves me?
