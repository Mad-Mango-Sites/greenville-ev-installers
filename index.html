Find installers in your town

Your town changes more than you'd think: who your power company is, which office issues the permit, and whether you can get Duke Energy's credit.

  • Greenville: City permits inside city limits, county permits outside. Mostly Duke Energy.
  • Greer: Many homes are on Greer CPW, so no Duke credit for those addresses.
  • Simpsonville: Duke Energy or Laurens Electric Cooperative, depending on the street.
  • Mauldin: City of Mauldin permits. Duke or Laurens Electric.
  • Taylors: No city government, so Greenville County issues permits.
  • Travelers Rest: Duke or Blue Ridge Electric Cooperative. City permits are filed online.
  • Fountain Inn: City permits through an online portal. Duke or Laurens Electric.
  • Easley: Easley Combined Utilities serves most of the city. Pickens County, not Greenville County.

Four things to know before you call an installer

  1. Your power company decides your credit. Duke Energy customers can apply for the Charger Prep Credit, which pays toward the wiring and panel work. For most Upstate homes the cap is $1,236. Homes on Greer CPW, Easley Combined Utilities, Blue Ridge Electric or Laurens Electric can't use Duke's credit.
  2. Your address decides your permit office. A Greenville mailing address doesn't mean you're inside city limits. Outside a city, Greenville County Building Safety issues the permit.
  3. Your panel decides most of the price. The charger itself is often the smaller part of the bill. A long wire run or a panel upgrade costs more.
  4. The federal tax credit is gone. The 30C credit doesn't apply to chargers placed in service after June 30, 2026.

Common questions

How much does it cost to install an EV charger in Greenville?

It depends mostly on your panel and how far the charger is from it. One local installer, Enhanced Electric, publishes its prices: $750 to $1,500 to install a Tesla Wall Connector and $500 to $900 for a NEMA 14-50 outlet (as listed on its site in Oct 2026). Jobs that need a panel upgrade cost more. Our cost guide explains what moves the price.

Do I need a permit?

Yes. A new 240-volt circuit for a charger is electrical work, and the cities and the county here require a permit for it. Your installer normally pulls it. If you plan to apply for Duke's Charger Prep Credit, you'll need a copy of the approved permit anyway.

Does Duke Energy pay for the charger?

No. Duke's Charger Prep Credit covers the prep work: wiring, conduit, the outlet, and panel or breaker work. Duke's own page says the charger hardware, mounting and permit fees don't count.

Are these installers licensed?

We show a license number only when the business publishes one on its own site, and we're checking each against the South Carolina Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation (LLR) lookup. Before you hire anyone, ask for their license number and proof of insurance.

Can I charge from a regular outlet instead?

Many EVs come with a cord that plugs into a standard 120-volt outlet. That's Level 1 charging. It's slow, but it can be enough if you drive short distances and park overnight. Most people who install a charger want Level 2, which needs a 240-volt circuit.
